Run your research on the cluster by saying what you need.

Your code already works on your own computer, often on a single GPU. Labmill takes it to your cluster: it finds the data, writes the job, runs it and brings the results back. You don't need to learn Slurm or the terminal.

From a sentence to results on your desk

Labmill runs on your computer and uses your own cluster account. Each step is shown to you before it happens.

  1. Say what you want

    "Run this on 4 GPUs overnight" is enough. Sample code without run scripts is fine too.

  2. It finds the data

    It searches the folders you chose and shows the files it found before anything moves.

  3. It copies the data

    Over SSH to the cluster, with checksums. A broken transfer starts again by itself.

  4. It runs the job

    It writes the job script, checks that Slurm accepts it, and submits it when you approve.

  5. It brings results back

    It watches the job, explains a failure in plain words, and checks every file it downloads.

You stay in control of your data and your account

Nothing moves without your OK
You approve the file list and the destination before data leaves your computer.
Every script is visible
You can read and edit each job script before it is submitted.
Your login stays yours
You sign in with your own cluster account and two-factor step. Passwords are never written to disk.
Nothing to install on the cluster
One program on your computer. Nothing is left running on the cluster when you are done.

It knows your cluster before you ask

Every cluster has its own partitions, storage rules and software. Labmill reads the public documentation of research computing centers and keeps each fact with its source and date. Real jobs make a fact more or less trusted, and Labmill tells you when it relies on a fact it is not sure of.

Labmill never receives your data files, code or passwords.

Jobs on the gpu partition can run for at most 3 days.

For research computing teams

Labmill answers the routine first-job questions: which partition, where to put data, why a job failed. The corporate tier is for institutions. It lets research software engineers and HPC staff see how their users' jobs go, correct the facts about their own clusters and contact users who are stuck, so your team has time for the hard problems.
