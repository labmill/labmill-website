Short version

  • Labmill never receives your data files, your code, your cluster passwords or your two-factor codes.
  • If you use the Labmill service, we keep your sign-in data, your job records and a few redacted log lines of failed jobs.
  • By default your redacted job records also help Labmill learn which settings work on which clusters. You can opt out. The opt-out plan costs 5 times the personal price.
  • If you join an institution, the research software engineers (RSEs) and IT staff of that institution see the jobs of all its users.
  • You can delete your account. Contact: help@labmill.org.

What the Labmill client does on your computer

The client runs on your computer. It connects to your clusters with your own login. Transfers go straight between your computer and your cluster, or through an S3 bucket that you name. Cluster passwords and two-factor codes are not written to disk and are not sent to us.

The assistant in the client is Claude, a model of Anthropic. When you chat, the client sends your messages and the context the assistant needs to a model service. This context can include job states, resource use, the end of a log, and the text of a file that the assistant reads for you. The client asks you before it reads a file that can hold keys or tokens, because the content goes to the model. By default the client calls the Anthropic API with your own Anthropic key, and then Anthropic's terms and privacy policy apply to that traffic. If you use the Labmill relay, the model calls go through our service (see "Processors"). [OWNER: confirm which route is live at launch: your own key, the Labmill relay, or both.]

Public research profile (only with your consent)

At sign-up we show which public sources we may read, for example ORCID, publication lists, GitHub and a faculty page. We read them only if you tick the checkbox. We use them to find your institution, field and experience. You can see, correct and delete this profile at any time.

Your institutions and field

You tell us the institutions and HPC centers where you have accounts. If you join an institution's team, you confirm an email address at one of its domains with a code. We may store your field of science from a public list. A narrow subfield is shared only when many users have it (see below).

Job records

After a job changes state, the client can send a job record. It holds numbers and categories: the cluster, partition and constraints, requested and used CPU, memory, GPUs and time, state, exit code, queue wait, failure category, and the names and versions of software the job used. It does not hold the job name, folder or file names, the command line, project or grant names, your data or your code.

Redacted log lines

For a failed job only, the client can send a few log lines around the error. The client removes secrets, paths, user and host names, IP addresses, email addresses and long numbers, and also every job, folder, file and project name that it knows. The server removes the generic items again. Risk: an error line can still name a gene, a compound or a project. If your work is confidential, use the per-job "do not share" switch or the opt-out plan. Do not rely on the redaction alone.

The learning pool and the opt-out

By default your job records help the shared learning pool, without your user ID. A group is counted only when at least 3 users contribute, so one person cannot be singled out. A software package or subfield enters the pool only when it is common (at least 10 users at 3 institutions; these numbers can change). Until then it is visible only to you and, for software, to the RSEs of your institution.

You can opt out in the settings. Your records are then not used in the pool. The opt-out plan costs 5 times the personal price. Marking a single job "do not share" counts as an opt-out for that job. If you opt out after you paid, the price changes at the next billing step [OWNER: confirm how and when]. Records that already went into anonymous statistics stay in them, without your identity. [OWNER: confirm.]

Institutions and RSE visibility

If you join an institution's team (corporate plan), the RSEs and IT staff of that institution who are team administrators see the jobs of all users of that institution and the names and email addresses of its members. They can correct the institution's site facts, see failure trends, and send you messages. We tell you this at sign-up, at the join step and at the first run of the client. Raw job records and log lines stay inside the institution's team. Only general lessons and anonymous statistics go to the shared pool. An institution in the EU can choose that only users who opt in are visible. [OWNER: confirm this option is built.]

If you use Labmill with a personal card and no institution, no institution sees your jobs.

Account deletion

You can delete your account in the app. Your name and email are removed at once, and sign-in, tokens and consents end. We remove the rest within 30 days: log lines after 1 year at the latest and job records after 3 years at the latest, as in the table above [OWNER: confirm whether you want to delete all job records inside the 30 days]. Backups age out within 35 days. What your records already added to anonymous statistics and to confirmed facts stays, without your identity. An active or past-due subscription must be canceled first. You can also write to help@labmill.org. A minimal empty account row stays, so that old records cannot be linked to a new person.

Security

Traffic uses HTTPS. Access to data is limited by account and team. The client stores refresh tokens in your operating system's key store. No system is fully secure. Report a problem to help@labmill.org. [OWNER: add the breach notice process.]

The LabmillBot crawler

LabmillBot reads public documentation pages of research computing systems. It obeys robots.txt and stores facts, short quotes and the source link, not whole pages. It is not meant to collect personal data. If a page holds personal data, write to bot@labmill.org. More: the LabmillBot page.
