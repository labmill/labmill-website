Short version

  • Labmill does not receive your data files, your program code, your job scripts, your cluster passwords or your two-factor codes.
  • If you use the Labmill service, we keep your sign-in data, your job records and redacted log lines of failed jobs. The current client does not send job records yet.
  • By default your redacted job records also help Labmill learn which settings work on which clusters. You can opt out. A fully private account (the opt-out plan) costs 35 USD a month instead of 6 USD.
  • If you join an institution's team and the institution allows it, its research software engineers (RSEs) and team administrators see your jobs, except the jobs you mark private.
  • You can delete your account. Contact: help@labmill.org.

What the Labmill client does on your computer

The client runs on your computer. It connects to your clusters with your own login. Transfers go straight between your computer and your cluster, or through an S3 bucket that you name. Cluster passwords and two-factor codes are not written to disk and are not sent to us.

The assistant in the client is Claude, a model of Anthropic. When you chat, the client sends your messages and the context the assistant needs to a model service. This context can include job states, resource use, the end of a log, and the text of a file that the assistant reads for you. The client asks you before it reads a file that can hold keys or tokens, because the content goes to the model. The client calls the Anthropic API directly with your own Anthropic key, or a relay ("broker") that your institution runs, if you set one. This traffic does not go through Labmill's servers, and Anthropic's terms and privacy policy (or your institution's terms) apply to it. The Labmill service also has a model relay for a small number of fixed tasks (plan a job, explain a failed job). It sends the text to the Anthropic API. The relay is not in use today, and the current client does not call it. [OWNER: confirm the model route at launch.]

Data we collect

Sign-in data

You sign in with Google or GitHub. [OWNER: ORCID and LinkedIn sign-in are in the code but not turned on; confirm the provider list at launch.] We keep from the provider your name, email address, whether the provider says the email is verified, and a provider account ID. For Google we ask for the scopes openid email profile only. For GitHub we read your primary verified email address. We do not receive your provider password.

An account has a session cookie that is needed for sign-in (secure, HTTP-only, valid up to 30 days). Program tokens that you make in the settings for the client are kept only as hashes. They end after 1 year, and you can revoke them in the settings. We set no advertising or tracking cookies.

Public research profile (only with your consent)

At sign-up we show which public sources we may read, for example ORCID, publication lists, GitHub and a faculty page. We read them only if you tick the checkbox. We use them to find your institution, field and experience. You can see, correct and delete this profile at any time.

Your institutions and field

You tell us the institutions and HPC centers where you have accounts. If you join an institution's team, you confirm an email address at one of its domains with a code. A job record can hold your field of science. [OWNER: the design keeps a narrow subfield on your computer until many users have it (see below); the service does not check this yet.]

Job records

The service can receive a job record after a job changes state. The current client does not send job records yet. A job record holds numbers and categories: the cluster, partition and constraints, requested and used resources (CPU, memory, GPUs and time), state, exit code, queue wait, failure category, field of science, whether the job is shared, and the names and versions of software the job used. It does not hold the job script, the job name, folder or file names, the command line, project or grant names, your data or your code. The service refuses a job record with any other field. [OWNER: a design proposal adds the redacted job script and resource samples; if this is built, this section must change first.]

Redacted log lines

For a failed job only (failed, timed out or out of memory), the client can send up to 1,000 redacted lines of the job log. The client removes secrets, paths, user and host names, IP addresses, email addresses and long numbers, and also every job, folder, file and project name that it knows. The server removes the generic items again. Risk: an error line can still name a gene, a compound or a project. If your work is confidential, use the per-job "do not share" switch or the opt-out plan. Do not rely on the redaction alone.

  • To bill you and to prevent abuse.
  • To build a knowledge base and statistics: which settings work on which clusters, common failure causes, and how often software is used. We use retrieval and statistics. We do not train models on your data. [OWNER: confirm this wording.]

The learning pool and the opt-out

By default your job records help the shared learning pool, without your user ID. A group is counted only when at least 3 users contribute, so one person cannot be singled out. A software package or subfield enters the pool only when it is common (at least 10 users at 3 institutions; these numbers can change). Until then it is visible only to you and, for software, to the RSEs of your institution.

You can opt out in the settings. Your records are then not used in the pool. A fully private account (the opt-out plan) costs 35 USD a month instead of 6 USD. Marking a single job "do not share" (private) keeps that job out of the pool. Today it does not change your price. [OWNER: confirm.] If you opt out after you paid, Settings asks you to move the subscription to the new price; Stripe then bills the difference for the rest of the period (proration). [OWNER: confirm.] Records that already went into anonymous statistics stay in them, without your identity. [OWNER: confirm.]

Institutions and RSE visibility

If you join an institution's team (corporate plan), the RSEs and team administrators of that institution see the names and email addresses of its members. They see your jobs only when the institution set its team to "all users", and then only the jobs that you did not mark private. For a private job they see only counts per day and state, and they can ask you for access. In a team set to "opt-in" (the default), they do not see your jobs. [OWNER: a switch for each user to opt in is in the settings, but it does not yet open jobs to the RSEs in an opt-in team.] RSEs always see the help requests and messages that you send them. They can correct the institution's site facts, see failure trends, and send you messages. Raw job records and log lines stay inside the institution's team. Only general lessons and anonymous statistics go to the shared pool.

If you use Labmill with a personal card and no institution, no institution sees your jobs.

Account deletion

You can delete your account in Settings in the app. At once, your name and email are removed, your sign-in links, tokens, consents and team memberships end, and your jobs leave the learning pool. 30 days later we remove your job records, log lines, model use, subscription records, verified institution emails and invitations. [OWNER: the removal step is built but does not run on a schedule yet; make it run daily before real users.] Backups age out after 35 more days. What your records already added to anonymous statistics and to confirmed facts stays, without your identity. An active or past-due subscription must be canceled first. You can also write to help@labmill.org. A minimal empty account row stays, so that old records cannot be linked to a new person.

Security

Traffic uses HTTPS. Access to data is limited by account and team. The client keeps no Labmill sign-in; it reads your Anthropic key from the environment and does not write it to disk. No system is fully secure. Report a problem to help@labmill.org. [OWNER: add the breach notice process.]

The LabmillBot crawler

LabmillBot reads public documentation pages of research computing systems. It obeys robots.txt and stores facts, short quotes and the source link, not whole pages. It is not meant to collect personal data. If a page holds personal data, write to bot@labmill.org. More: the LabmillBot page.
